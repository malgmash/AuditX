# Northwind Systems expense and timesheet policy

This policy applies to everyone who claims expenses or submits a timesheet. It describes what
the company reimburses, what it needs to see, and how submissions are reviewed. It is the
document the review system quotes when someone asks why a claim was flagged.

Nothing in this policy decides an individual case. A flag raised by the review system is a
prompt for a person to look, never a conclusion about the person who submitted it.

## Submitting an expense

Submit a claim within thirty days of the date on the receipt. Claims older than ninety days
need a manager's written approval before they can be reimbursed.

Every claim needs the merchant, the date the money was spent, the category, the amount, and a
receipt image. Claims of twenty-five dollars or less may be submitted without a receipt image
if the description says what was bought and why.

Submit each receipt once. A receipt that covers more than one person or more than one category
is still a single claim; split it in the description rather than submitting it twice. If a
claim is returned for a correction, edit the original rather than submitting a second one.

## Receipts

A receipt image must show the merchant name, the date, and the total. A photograph of a printed
receipt is acceptable. A photograph of a screen showing a receipt is acceptable where no
printed copy exists.

Keep the original for ninety days after reimbursement. Finance may ask to see it.

## Per diem and meal limits

Meals while travelling are reimbursed up to seventy-five dollars a day, covering all meals for
that day. The limit is a daily total, not a per-meal limit.

Client entertainment is not covered by the meal limit. It has no fixed ceiling and needs the
names of the people present in the description and approval from a department head for amounts
above five hundred dollars.

Alcohol is reimbursed only as part of client entertainment.

## Travel and lodging

Book travel through the company travel desk where one exists for your destination. Economy
fares are the standard for flights under six hours.

Lodging is reimbursed at the actual room rate plus tax. Room service is claimed as a meal and
counts toward the daily meal limit.

Mileage in a personal vehicle is reimbursed at the published rate for the year. Log the
journey, not the fuel.

## Equipment and software

Equipment above four hundred dollars needs approval before purchase. Software subscriptions
need approval before the first payment, not at renewal.

A recurring subscription charged monthly is a single approved expense, and each month's charge
is claimed against that approval.

## Training and conferences

Conference tickets, course fees and professional certification are reimbursed in full with
prior approval from a department head. There is no ceiling on the amount, and a large single
charge for a conference ticket is expected rather than unusual.

Travel and meals during a conference follow the ordinary travel and meal sections of this
policy.

## Categories

Choose the category that matches what was bought, not the merchant's main line of business. A
laptop bought from a shop that mostly sells stationery is Equipment, not Supplies.

If no category fits, choose Other and say what it was in the description. Other is the right
answer when the category is unclear.

## Timesheets

Submit a timesheet each week, by the Monday following the week it covers.

Log the hours you worked, the project, and the location you worked from. Where the location
differs from your usual office, say so: a mismatch between a logged location and a receipt from
elsewhere on the same day is one of the things the review system looks at, and a line in the
note field resolves it.

Do not log hours on a company holiday without a note recording who approved the work.

A working day of more than sixteen hours, or a week of more than eighty, will be flagged for a
conversation about workload.

## How submissions are reviewed

An automated system checks every submission against the published rules. It groups related
flags into a case for one reviewer to look at as a whole.

The system ranks work for a reviewer. It does not approve, decline, or pay anything, and it
applies no penalty of its own. Every outcome is decided by a person, and that decision is
recorded with the reviewer's name.

Where a claim has not yet been paid, a reviewer may pause the reimbursement while a case is
open. Pay is never affected, and a paused reimbursement is released as soon as the case is
closed in the employee's favour.

## Asking about a flag

You can see your own flags, the reason for each, and the rule that raised it. If something
looks wrong, reply on the case with what actually happened. Most flags are resolved by one
sentence of context that the system had no way to know.

A flag is not an accusation and is not recorded against you once a reviewer has closed it.
